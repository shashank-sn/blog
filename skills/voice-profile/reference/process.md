# Traits to measure

Measure each trait across the samples. Give a number where you can, and one quoted example.

| Trait | How to measure |
|---|---|
| Sentence length | Mean words per sentence, and the spread (standard deviation) |
| Rhythm | How often a very short sentence (under 6 words) follows a long one |
| Paragraph shape | Mean sentences per paragraph; share of one-sentence paragraphs |
| Openings | The most common first words of sentences and of pieces |
| Endings | How pieces end: a question, a line on its own, a call to act, a plain stop |
| Vocabulary | The 20 most frequent content words; words the writer never uses that are common elsewhere |
| Point of view | Share of "i", "we", "you" |
| Punctuation | Rate per 1,000 words of `?`, `!`, `:`, `;`, dashes, parentheses |
| Case | Sentence case, or all lowercase |
| Questions | Questions per 100 sentences |
| Banned constructions | Patterns that never appear, for example "it's not X, it's Y", or stacked hedges |

## Pattern findings

Flag these in a draft, each with the sentence quoted:

- Stacked hedges ("it may potentially help").
- Signposts that announce a point instead of making it ("here's the thing").
- Three-item lists after a colon, used by habit.
- Paragraphs that end on a slogan with no new fact.
- Marketing adjectives with no measurement.

## Readiness

A sample is ready when it is published, at least 150 words (or the channel's normal length), in the right format, and recent enough to represent the voice the person wants now.
